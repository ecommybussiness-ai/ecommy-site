Amazon account health checklist for Indian sellers.

Seven practical weekly checks to keep your seller operations organised, customer-ready and easier to manage.

Amazon account health is not a task to open only when something goes wrong. A simple weekly routine helps sellers spot listing gaps, order issues and customer-service tasks before they become bigger operational problems. This checklist is designed for Indian sellers who want a clear, repeatable process—not a shortcut around Amazon policy.

1. Check Account Health notifications first

Start each review by checking notifications, performance messages and action items inside Seller Central. Read the exact wording before making changes. A notice may relate to a product page, fulfilment, customer experience, documentation or a policy-related concern. Identify the affected SKU, ASIN or process first; avoid broad changes across every listing.

Keep a small internal log with the date, issue, owner, action taken and next follow-up date. It prevents duplicated work when more than one person supports an account.

2. Review priority listings for accuracy

A listing must clearly match the product being sold. Review titles, brand details, pack size, variations, key features, images and descriptions. The page must not create a different expectation from the actual product or offer. Check that the primary image, price and fulfilment promise are current.

  • Confirm the title identifies the correct product and variation.
  • Ensure images show the actual product accurately.
  • Check category, attributes and compliance-related details where applicable.
  • Remove outdated offers, discontinued pack sizes and conflicting content.

Listing quality is not only an SEO task. Clear information can reduce avoidable customer questions, cancellation risk and return-related confusion.

3. Check stock, pricing and buyability

A product cannot convert when it is out of stock, priced incorrectly or not eligible for the fulfilment option a shopper expects. For priority SKUs, review available inventory, inbound stock, suppressed offers, pricing changes and buyability alerts. Do not keep sending advertising traffic to an offer that is not ready.

4. Review orders, cancellations and returns

Look for patterns rather than isolated events. If cancellations, returns or delivery-related complaints increase for one SKU, investigate the listing, packaging, inventory process, price expectation and fulfilment setup. A recurring issue usually needs a process correction, not just a reply to the latest customer message.

5. Handle buyer messages carefully

Buyer messages may contain personal information. Access them only through authorised Seller Central access, use an approved team member, and keep replies focused on the order or customer query. Avoid moving buyer data into personal WhatsApp chats, public documents or unnecessary spreadsheets. Create a simple internal escalation path for delivery disputes, product defects and refund-related questions.

6. Compare advertising activity with listing readiness

Before increasing advertising budgets, confirm that the advertised product is in stock and its page is ready to convert. Review search terms, spend, attributed sales, price changes and the landing-page experience together. A weak listing, stock-out or uncompetitive offer can make a relevant campaign look inefficient. 7. Plan next week's actions

End the review with a short action list. Give each item one responsible person and a realistic due date. Examples include correcting a title, checking an inventory discrepancy, updating a product image, replying to an operational message or reviewing search terms for a priority ASIN.

  • Monday: notifications, account health and order exceptions.
  • Mid-week: listings, inventory and pricing checks.
  • Friday: advertising review, customer-service follow-up and next-week plan.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not ignore messages until month-end, give multiple people shared login credentials, or make hurried catalogue edits without checking the live product page. Avoid claims that cannot be supported, and do not treat one account-health notice as a reason to alter unrelated listings. A controlled process is safer and easier to measure than reactive bulk edits.

Frequently asked questions

How often should an Amazon seller check account health?

A focused weekly review is a practical minimum. Urgent notifications, order issues and buyer messages should be monitored more frequently according to your operating volume.

Can a listing affect account performance?

Yes. Accurate product information, images and offer details can help shoppers make informed decisions and reduce avoidable post-purchase confusion.

What should I do after an Amazon notification?

Read the exact notification, identify the relevant product or process, correct the underlying issue where appropriate and retain a record of the action taken. Use current Seller Central guidance for the specific notice.
